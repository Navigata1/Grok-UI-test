# The first 30 seconds and the retention map

Storytelling is packaging that keeps its promise. The open has one job: prove, inside ten seconds, that the click was right.

## The first 30 seconds

1. Seconds 0 to 5: show or state the promise from the title and thumbnail. The viewer must see the thing they clicked for.
2. Seconds 5 to 15: the stake or the question. Why this matters, what could go wrong, what you did not expect.
3. Seconds 15 to 30: the roadmap in one line, then start. No channel intro, no "before we begin", no logo animation.

Write it word for word. Read it to someone who has not seen the title: if they cannot tell you the promise, rewrite.

## The retention map

| Timestamp | Beat | Device | What the viewer gets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Open | promise + stake | the reason they clicked |
| ~1:00 | Rehook 1 | new question or escalation | a reason to stay past the first dip |
| ~2:30 | Payoff 1 | first result | proof the video delivers |
| every 60 to 90s | Rehook | reveal, twist, countdown, contrast | a reason to stay |
| final third | Biggest payoff | the thumbnail moment | the promise, kept |
| end | Bridge | the next question | a reason to watch the sequel |

Rules: the thumbnail moment happens on screen, not just in the title. Every dip longer than 20 seconds in the retention graph is a section to cut or re-hook next time. Ordered structures (steps, ranks, countdowns) hold better than unordered ones.

## The film-school habit

Shoot the ending first so the open can tease it honestly. Cut arrivals and goodbyes. Show the change, not the setup.
